EU Rewrites the CSRD Rulebook: Revised ESRS and Voluntary Standard Now Official

Sep 21, 2026 2 min

Two significant pieces of secondary legislation completing the Omnibus I simplification package for corporate sustainability reporting were published in the Official Journal of the European Union today.

What was published

The European Commission published two Delegated Regulations today:

  • Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1563, which amends Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772 and replaces the current European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) with a revised, simplified set — referred to hereafter as the Revised ESRS.
  • Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1560, which establishes a voluntary sustainability reporting standard for undertakings outside the scope of the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD).

Revised ESRS: Key Dates and Transitional Options

The Revised ESRS enters into force on 10 November 2026 and becomes mandatory for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2027 — meaning first reports under the new standard are due in 2028.

Undertakings already within scope of the CSRD for a financial year beginning in 2026 have a one-time transitional choice. They may apply:

  1. The existing ESRS (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772) in its current form;
  2. The existing ESRS supplemented by targeted reliefs drawn from the new Regulation, covering among other things the top-down materiality approach, value chain data limitations, treatment of new acquisitions and disposals, Taxonomy presentation, and the executive summary; or
  3. The Revised ESRS in full.

Whichever version is applied, the sustainability statement must explicitly state which set of standards was used.

Voluntary Reporting Standard: Who It Applies To

The voluntary standard enters into force on 24 September 2026 and is available immediately to any undertaking not subject to mandatory CSRD reporting requirements.

The Regulation also introduces a value chain data cap, effective from financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2027. From that point, undertakings subject to mandatory sustainability reporting may not require, from value chain partners with an average of 1,000 employees or fewer in the preceding financial year, information beyond the essential environmental, social, and governance datapoints defined in Annex II to the Regulation provided the request is for the requester's own CSRD compliance purposes. This cap does not restrict information requests required under other EU or national legal obligations.

What This Means in Practice

The publication of these two Regulations completes a key pillar of the EU's Omnibus simplification agenda and brings meaningful relief to companies that found the original ESRS overly burdensome. The revised framework reduces mandatory disclosure requirements while maintaining the integrity of the reporting system. For smaller undertakings in corporate supply chains, the value chain cap provides an important layer of protection against disproportionate data requests from larger reporting entities.

Companies currently preparing FY2026 sustainability statements should review the transitional options carefully and document their chosen approach in their sustainability statement.
